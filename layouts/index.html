Numerical methods, in pure Go.
Fast, and mathematically right.

go-numerics is the numerical layer of the Go scientific stack built on go-ndarray: optimisation and root finding, quadrature and ODE solvers, and special functions. Correct: every result is compared with an answer computed independently — SciPy, the netlib Fortran, and mpmath at 192 and 320 bits — and errors are measured, in ulps where it matters. Fast: timed against SciPy on one core, and faster on every case below. No cgo, no assembly.

Why go-numerics

The numerical calls notebooks make most.

A census of 339 scientific notebooks set optimize's order — minimize, minimize_scalar, scalar roots, root, least_squares and curve_fit, linprog — and integrate and special cover quad, solve_ivp and the functions every statistic is computed with. Each Go function names the reference call it corresponds to.

📉

Optimisation and roots

Nelder–Mead, Powell, CG, BFGS, Newton-CG and L-BFGS-B; SLSQP and an interior-point method (trust-constr) with bounds, equality and inequality constraints; quadratic programming by a Goldfarb–Idnani active set and OSQP's ADMM; Brent, Ridder and Newton for scalar roots; MINPACK's hybrid method for systems.

📈

Least squares and fits

Levenberg–Marquardt and trust-region reflective with bounds and robust losses; CurveFit with the parameter covariance, the model per point or over arrays (Model.Vec, one call per sweep as SciPy's); linear programming with duals.

∫

Quadrature

QUADPACK ported from the netlib Fortran: singularities, infinite ranges, oscillatory and algebraic weights, Cauchy principal values; double, triple and N-dimensional integrals.

🌀

ODE solvers

RK45, RK23, DOP853, Radau and BDF with events and dense output, and an automatic Runge–Kutta/BDF switch for problems that turn stiff.

Γ

Special functions

Gamma, beta, erf and the normal distribution, incomplete gamma and beta and their inverses, Bessel functions of real order, zeta, elliptic integrals — and special/cmplx, a drop-in math/cmplx whose every part is measured in ulps against mpmath (Tanh(400+1i) is 1, not NaN).

🧮

On go-ndarray

Curve-fit data, covariances, LP matrices and ODE solutions are *ndarray.Array; special/nd applies a function element-wise with broadcasting, across cores.

📦

Pure Go, CGO=0

No cgo, no assembly. CI runs the tests on amd64 and arm64 and under emulation on riscv64, loong64, ppc64le and s390x.

⚖️

BSD-3-Clause

Every repository of the organization. Ported code keeps its attribution in place.

Speed

Faster than SciPy on every case below, not yet on every function.

Medians of interleaved Go and SciPy 1.18.1 runs on one core of a shared 16-core Apple M4 Max, from each library's docs/perf.md; the rows marked refbench were started below a 1-minute load of 8, the others on a loaded host. SciPy's numbers include its Python callbacks, which is most of its time on small problems; the documentation gives them without, too. No case below is slower than SciPy today. The functions that still are, among them the integer-order Bessel functions, the incomplete beta function and Softmax, are listed with their ratios in special#33.
